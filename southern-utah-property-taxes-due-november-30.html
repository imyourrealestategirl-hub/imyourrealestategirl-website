Southern Utah Property Taxes Are Due Nov. 30: What to Check

By Alecia Jones · November 18, 2026

The short answer

If you own a home in Washington County or Iron County, your 2026 property tax bill is due Monday, November 30. If you have a mortgage, your lender probably pays it from your escrow account, but it’s worth a two-minute check. Pay late and you’ll owe a penalty of at least $10. While the bill is in front of you, look for the one thing most people skip: whether your home is getting Utah’s 45% primary residential exemption. If it isn’t, you could be paying a lot more than the family next door. And if you or your parents are on a fixed income, there’s tax help out there with deadlines you’ll want on the calendar now.

Does my mortgage company pay my property taxes?

If your loan has an escrow account, usually yes. Washington County mails the tax notice to you and sends the information to your mortgage company. But “usually” isn’t “always.” Take a quick look if any of these apply to you:

  • You paid off your home this year. The escrow is gone, so the bill is now yours to pay.
  • You refinanced or your loan was sold to a new servicer this year. Things can slip through during a handoff.
  • You bought a home this year. Taxes were split at closing, but double-check who’s paying the full 2026 bill.
  • You have a second home or rental without an escrow account.

Your servicer’s website should show the payment, or look up your parcel with the county treasurer.

What happens if I pay late?

Under state law, the penalty is 2.5% of the unpaid tax or $10, whichever is greater, per parcel. If you pay everything by January 31, it drops to 1% (still with a $10 minimum). After January 31, interest kicks in, charged back to January 1, at a rate between 7% and 10%.

Washington County takes partial payments of $10 or more, so if money is tight, paying something is better than nothing. Just know they don’t set up formal payment plans on delinquent taxes. And a home that stays delinquent for five years can end up in the county’s May tax sale, which nobody wants.

What is the 45% primary residential exemption, and do I have it?

This is the part I most want you to check. In Utah, the home you live in full-time gets a 45% break on its value for tax purposes. You’re taxed on 55% of what the county says it’s worth, and it covers the house plus up to one acre.

Washington County gives this example on its residential exemption page, using a sample 1.1% tax rate on a $350,000 home:

  • With the exemption: $350,000 × 55% = $192,500 taxable, about $2,117.50 a year
  • Without it: about $3,850 a year

That’s more than $1,700 a year on one house. Here’s how to check yours: find the market value and the taxable value on your notice. If the taxable value is about 55% of market value, you’re set. If they’re the same, call the assessor.

In Washington County, the owner has to apply. You can do it online through the assessor or call 435-301-7020 for a paper form. In Iron County, call the assessor’s office to confirm how your home is classified. Second homes, cabins, and vacation properties don’t qualify, and it’s limited to one per household.

I see this come up most with people who just moved here, people who used to rent out their place and moved back in, and folks who bought a home from an out-of-state owner.

Why did my bill go up if the county didn’t raise taxes?

Fair question. Washington County kept its own tax rate flat in 2025 for the 16th year in a row (Washington County). But the county is only one line on your bill. Your school district, city, and special districts (water, fire, mosquito abatement and others) each set their own rates.

Utah uses something called a certified tax rate, which generally adjusts rates down as values go up so each entity collects about the same money as last year, plus new growth. So your bill can still go up if:

  • Your home’s value rose faster than the average home around you
  • Voters approved a bond, like for new schools
  • A city or district held a “truth in taxation” hearing and raised its rate
  • You lost the primary residential exemption

Can I still appeal my home’s value?

Not for 2026. Both counties’ appeal windows closed in September. Iron County lists September 15 as its deadline, and Washington County’s page now says the 2026 period has closed.

For next year: value notices go out in late July. If the number looks high, you’ll have a short window to file with the county Board of Equalization. Your best evidence is recent sales of similar homes near you, and that’s something I can pull for you, free. Just text me when your notice comes.

Is there property tax help for seniors, veterans, and people on a tight budget?

Yes, and a lot of people who qualify never apply. These come from the Utah State Tax Commission’s Publication 36:

  • Homeowner’s credit (circuit breaker): up to $1,412 off your tax for homeowners 67+ (or an unmarried surviving spouse) with 2025 household income under $44,221. Apply with the county by September 1.
  • Renter refund: up to $1,412 for renters 66+ under the same income limit. Apply with the Tax Commission by December 31. If you have a parent or grandparent renting here, send this one to them now.
  • Disabled veterans: an exemption scaled to your disability rating, starting at 10% service-connected.
  • Blind exemption: up to $11,500 of taxable value, no income or age test. File by September 1.
  • Senior deferral: for owners 75+ who qualify, taxes can be put off (interest still applies). Apply by September 1.

In Washington County these run through the Clerk-Auditor (435-301-7220). In Iron County, the Tax Commission lists 435-477-8330.

Your 10-minute property tax checklist

  1. Find your 2026 tax notice, or look up your parcel on the county treasurer’s site.
  2. Confirm who’s paying: you or your mortgage escrow.
  3. Check that your taxable value is about 55% of market value.
  4. Pay by Monday, November 30, or confirm your lender did.
  5. Put “watch for value notice” on your calendar for late July 2027.
  6. If you or a parent might qualify for relief, put September 1 on the calendar too.

Frequently asked questions

When are property taxes due in St. George and Cedar City? November 30 each year. In 2026 that’s a Monday.

I never got a tax notice. Am I off the hook? No. Taxes are due November 30 whether or not the notice arrived. Look up your parcel with the county treasurer.

How do I know if I have the primary residential exemption? Your taxable value should be about 55% of your market value. If not, call your county assessor.

Can I appeal my value now? Not for 2026. Watch for your value notice in late July and file with the Board of Equalization before the deadline on it.

Do property taxes go up when home values go up? Not automatically. Utah’s certified tax rate usually adjusts rates down as values rise, but your share can still change.
