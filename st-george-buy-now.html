"Should I just wait?" is the question I hear most

I've lived in Southern Utah for over 20 years and I've been helping people buy and sell homes in the St. George area for more than 10. Almost every buyer I talk to this fall asks some version of the same thing: shouldn't I just wait until rates come down?

I get it. Nobody likes seeing a 7 in front of their interest rate. But here's what I tell my own clients. When rates finally drop, everyone who has been waiting jumps back in at the same time. Inventory gets snapped up, multiple offers come back, and sellers stop paying for anything.

Right now in St. George, we have the opposite of that.

The real numbers (minus the scary headlines)

Rates: Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.32% for the week ending October 22, 2026. A year ago it was about 6.34%. You can check the latest number on Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey.

Inventory: Active listings in the St. George metro climbed from about 1,644 in January 2026 to roughly 2,000 by May, and stayed there through the summer (FRED, St. George active listings).

Buydowns: the secret weapon most buyers never ask for

A buydown is when someone pays money upfront at closing to lower your interest rate. That someone can be the seller, a builder, or you. Right now a lot of St. George sellers and builders are willing to cover it, because they'd rather help with your rate than drop their price.

Temporary buydown (like a 2-1)

Your rate is 2% lower the first year and 1% lower the second year, then goes to the full rate. If rates drop in those two years, you can refinance before you ever hit the full payment.

Permanent buydown

You or the seller pay "discount points" to lock a lower rate for the life of the loan. It costs more upfront, but the savings never end.

Alecia's tip

When you write an offer, try asking for a buydown instead of a price cut. A lot of sellers say yes because their sale price stays the same on paper, and you get the bigger monthly savings.

How much can a seller contribute?

  • Conventional loans: 3% of the price with less than 10% down, 6% with 10% to 25% down, 9% with more than 25% down.
  • FHA loans: up to 6%.
  • VA loans: up to 4% in concessions, with some standard closing costs allowed on top.

Your lender will confirm the exact limit. For a plain-English guide to points, see the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

Why high inventory is your best friend

Remember 2021 and 2022? St. George homes were going under contract in a weekend, people were waiving inspections, and offers came in way over asking. I watched a lot of good families lose house after house. This market is different.

  • Time to think. See a home twice, bring family, and sleep on it without losing it.
  • Inspections that matter. Ask for repairs or credits instead of taking a house as-is.
  • Concessions on the table. Closing costs, buydowns, appliances, and HOA dues are all things I'm negotiating right now.
  • A better fit. No settling for the wrong neighborhood or floor plan just to get something.
Alecia's tip

Always check how long a home has been on the market. Once a St. George listing passes about 30 days, that seller is usually a lot more open to talking terms.

And the area keeps growing. Washington County is now the third most in-demand county in Utah, and the average listing price in south St. George went from about $364,500 in 2018 to about $622,000 in early 2026. That demand doesn't disappear. It waits for rates to drop, then comes back all at once.

Questions St. George buyers ask me all the time

Is it a good time to buy a house in St. George, Utah?

For a lot of buyers, yes. Rates are higher, but inventory is up and sellers are negotiating. If you plan to stay at least a few years and the payment fits your budget, this is one of the better buyer's windows St. George has had since before 2020.

Are home prices dropping in St. George?

Not in a big way. What's changed is how much sellers are willing to give. Most homes sell close to their final list price, but buyers are getting more in closing costs, buydowns, and repairs than they could a few years ago.

Will mortgage rates go down in 2027?

Nobody knows for sure, and anyone who says they do is guessing. That's why I like strategies that work either way. A buydown lowers your payment now, and if rates fall, you can refinance.

What is a 2-1 buydown?

A temporary rate reduction: 2% lower in year one, 1% lower in year two, then your full rate. The cost is paid upfront, often by the seller or builder.

Can I ask a St. George seller to pay for my buydown?

Yes. It's written into your offer as a seller concession. How much they can pay depends on your loan type and down payment.

Is a buydown better than a lower price?

Often, yes, if your monthly payment matters most. In the example above, the same $11,500 saved about $632 a month as a buydown versus about $70 a month as a price cut.

What if rates drop right after I buy?

Then you refinance and enjoy the lower rate. Talk with your lender about refinance costs ahead of time so you know your break-even point.

Are there down payment programs in St. George?

Yes. The Washington County Board of REALTORS® runs a REACH down payment assistance program, and Utah Housing Corporation offers loan programs for first-time and repeat buyers.
