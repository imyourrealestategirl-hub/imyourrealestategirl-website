Should I Sell My Home This Winter in Southern Utah?

By Alecia Jones · November 11, 2026

The short answer

Yes, you can sell a home in Southern Utah this winter, and for some sellers it’s the right call. Fewer homes compete with yours, St. George fills up with snowbirds and second-home shoppers, and the buyers who show up in December and January tend to be serious. The trade-off is real, though: winter homes usually take longer to sell and sell for a bit less than the same home would in spring. Last January, St. George homes took a median of 85 days to sell and closed at about 97.6% of list price. If you need to move, have a priced-right home, or want to beat the spring rush, winter works. If you can wait and want top dollar, plan to list in late February or March.

Why am I writing about winter selling now?

Because this is the time of year I start getting the same phone call. Someone’s thinking about selling, and they want to know if they should list now or “wait until spring like everybody else.” It’s a fair question, and the honest answer depends on your situation more than the calendar.

I’ve lived in Southern Utah for over 20 years, raised three kids in Cedar City, and I’ve been selling homes here for more than 10 of them. Winter in St. George and winter in Cedar City are two very different markets, and both are different from the national advice you’ll read online. So here’s how I’d think about it if you were my neighbor asking over the fence.

Do homes sell slower in winter in St. George?

Yes, usually. In January 2026, the median St. George home took 85 days to sell, and only about 1 in 6 (16.7%) sold within two weeks, according to Redfin data reported by Stacker. Nationally, the median was 66 days. Sellers here got about 97.6% of their list price on average, which tells me most homes sold with a little negotiation, not a bidding war.

That lines up with the bigger picture. HUD’s most recent market profile for the St. George area called the sales market “balanced” and showed about nine months of inventory in mid-2025, way up from under two months in 2021 (HUD USER). The same report showed the average existing-home price down about 7% from the year before. Buyers have choices again, and they know it.

So no, winter isn’t a season where you list on Friday and have five offers by Monday. But it’s also not dead. Homes sell every single week of the year here.

What’s different about selling in Cedar City in winter?

Cedar City is a smaller market, so a handful of sales can swing the numbers a lot from month to month. And we actually get winter up here. Snow on the driveway, icy steps, and short gray days all change how a home shows.

The upside in Cedar City is that winter buyers are almost always motivated. Think job transfers, Southern Utah University staff and faculty, and families who want to be settled before a new school term. People don’t usually tour homes in a snowstorm for fun.

Who is actually buying homes in December and January?

More people than you’d think, especially in Washington County. A 2019 Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute estimate reported by KUER counted nearly 29,000 seasonal residents in Washington County at peak times, and the water district has said roughly 20% of the county’s homes aren’t the owners’ primary residence (KUER). A lot of those folks rent here in the winter, fall in love with the sunshine, and start looking at homes while the weather back home is miserable.

Here’s who I typically see at winter showings:

  • Snowbirds and second-home buyers who are in town and can see homes in person.
  • Job relocations with a start date that doesn’t care about the season.
  • Retirees who want to be moved in before the next winter.
  • Families hoping to change schools at the semester break.
  • Buyers who lost out last spring and don’t want to compete again.

Will I get less for my home if I sell in winter?

Probably a little, and I’d rather tell you that now than surprise you later. A LendingTree study covered by the National Association of REALTORS® found January and February had the lowest median price per square foot nationally, while April through July were the most expensive months. The study estimated buyers could save about $23,000 on a 1,500-square-foot home by buying in January instead of May.

That’s a national number, not a Southern Utah one, and every home is different. But the direction is real. Spring brings more buyers, and more buyers means more leverage for sellers.

The flip side? Spring also brings a lot more listings. If your home is a common floor plan in a neighborhood with several homes for sale, you may stand out more in January than in April. That’s the part a lot of sellers miss.

One more thing to factor in: mortgage rates. Freddie Mac’s weekly average was 7.18% on November 5, down three weeks in a row, but still higher than the 6.22% from a year ago. If rates keep easing, more buyers will jump back in. Nobody can promise that, though, and I wouldn’t build your whole plan around a guess about rates. I’m not a lender, so talk to one for a real rate quote.

How do I get my home ready to show in winter?

Winter showings are about warmth and light. Buyers are coming in from the cold (or at least from a chilly St. George evening), and you want the house to feel like a place they’d want to land. My checklist:

  • Keep it warm. Set the thermostat a few degrees warmer than you normally would before showings.
  • Turn on every light. It gets dark by 5:30. Swap dim bulbs for bright, warm ones.
  • Clear snow and ice (Cedar City, this one’s for you). Shovel the walk, salt the steps, and keep a mat at the door.
  • Tone down the holiday decor. A wreath and a tree are lovely. Every surface covered in decorations makes rooms look smaller in photos.
  • Use photos that flatter the yard. If you have great summer or fall photos of your landscaping, we can use them.
  • Service the furnace and have the receipt handy. Buyers and inspectors will ask.
  • Fix the small stuff now. Fewer buyers means each one matters more.

I go deeper into prep in what to do before listing your home, and my winterizing guide for St. George and Cedar City homes covers pipes, sprinklers, and the stuff an inspector will notice.

What about the holidays? Should I take my home off the market?

I usually say no. Taking a home off the market resets the days-on-market clock in a lot of buyers’ minds, and the people shopping between Thanksgiving (November 26 this year) and New Year’s are often the most serious buyers of the whole year. You can block off a few days for family events. We just set showing windows that work for you.

If you’re not ready to list yet, the holidays are a great time to get a pricing opinion, make a short repair list, and be ready to hit the market the first week of January or in late February, before the spring crowd shows up.

Frequently asked questions

Is winter a bad time to sell a house in St. George? No. It’s slower, but St. George has a steady stream of winter buyers, including snowbirds and second-home shoppers. Homes that are priced right and show well still sell.

How long does it take to sell a house in St. George in winter? In January 2026, the median was 85 days, per Redfin data. Well-priced homes can go much faster.

Do homes sell for less in January? Nationally, January and February tend to have the lowest prices per square foot, according to a LendingTree study. Local results depend on your home, your price, and your competition.

Should I wait until spring to sell in Cedar City? If your timing is flexible and your home has outdoor features that show best without snow, spring may be better. If you need to move, winter buyers in Cedar City tend to be motivated.

What are mortgage rates right now? The 30-year fixed averaged 7.18% the week of November 5, 2026, per Freddie Mac. Talk to a lender for your own rate.

Can I list now and pause over Christmas? You can limit showings over specific days without taking the home off the market. We’ll work around your plans.
