Winterizing Your Southern Utah Home: St. George vs. Cedar City

By Alecia Jones · November 9, 2026

The short answer

If you live in Cedar City, your sprinklers should already be blown out and your outdoor faucets covered, because the average first frost there comes in late September. In St. George you have a little more breathing room, but the average first frost is around November 5, so now is the time. Drop to watering once a week at most in November, plan on almost no watering in December and January, and use the slow season to look at the grass-replacement rebates. Washington County is paying up to $3 a square foot right now.

Why I’m writing about sprinklers instead of houses

Most of the people who read this blog aren’t moving anytime soon. You’re my past clients, neighbors, friends from church and ball games, and people I’ve known for years. I’ve lived in Southern Utah for over 20 years and raised three kids here in Cedar City, and after 10+ years of walking through homes for a living, I can tell you the most expensive repairs I see often start with something small that got skipped in the fall.

Over the years, nothing has bothered me more than driving around town in the spring and watching neighbors dig up their front lawns to fix broken pipes. It’s a mess, it’s expensive, and most of the time it could have been avoided with a little prep in the fall.

A cracked backflow valve. A hose left on the spigot. A sprinkler running at 6 a.m. in December, making an ice rink of the sidewalk. None of it is dramatic, but all of it costs money, and most of it takes 20 minutes to prevent.

Is it too late to winterize my sprinklers?

In Cedar City, Enoch, and the higher areas

If you haven’t done it yet, do it this week. Cedar City sits at roughly 5,600 feet, and nights below freezing are normal by now. Water left sitting in the lines, valves, and especially the backflow preventer can freeze, expand, and crack parts. That usually isn’t discovered until you turn things on in April and see a geyser.

In St. George, Washington, Hurricane, and Ivins

Most of Washington County doesn’t blow out sprinkler lines the way Northern Utah does, because our hard freezes are short. But “short” isn’t “never.” Exposed pipes, above-ground backflow valves, and hose bibs on the north side of the house can still freeze on a cold January night. Insulate what’s exposed and keep an eye on the forecast.

The Utah State University Extension has a good guide on irrigation system maintenance if you want the details. If you’re not comfortable with an air compressor, call a sprinkler company. It’s usually a quick visit, and it’s cheaper than replacing a valve in the spring.

What should be on my November home checklist?

Here’s the list I’d hand a friend. Most of it works for both ends of the county line:

  • Unhook hoses and drain them. A hose left attached traps water in the faucet.
  • Cover outdoor faucets with foam covers. They cost a few dollars at any hardware store.
  • Insulate the backflow preventer and any exposed pipes, especially on the shady side of the house.
  • Change your sprinkler timer to the winter schedule, or turn it off (more on that below).
  • Change your furnace filter and have the furnace checked if it’s been a few years.
  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide detectors. Heating season is when CO detectors earn their keep.
  • Clean rain gutters and make sure downspouts point away from the foundation. Our storms are rare but heavy.
  • Look at the roof from the ground. Loose tiles or shingles are easier to fix before a windy storm.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is. If a pipe ever does burst, you’ll want to find it in 30 seconds, not 10 minutes.

Cedar City folks, add one more: have a snow shovel and ice melt ready before the first storm, not after.

How often should I water in winter in Southern Utah?

Less than you think. The Washington County Water Conservancy District’s watering guide recommends:

SeasonLawn watering (Washington County)
Spring (Mar–Apr)Up to 3 days per week
Summer (May–Aug)Up to 4 days per week
Fall (Sep–Oct)Up to 3 days per week
Winter (Nov–Feb)Up to 1 day per week; most plants don’t need irrigation in December and January

When you do water, the district recommends “cycle and soak”: three short cycles with about an hour between them, so the water soaks in instead of running down the gutter.

For Iron County, the Utah Division of Water Resources has said its weekly lawn watering guide recommends that most of the state shut off irrigation until spring (Utah Division of Water Resources). Check your water provider for any local rules that apply to your street.

Here’s the part I like: cutting back now lowers your water bill and helps the whole community. Water is the issue that shapes how fast Southern Utah can grow, and every one of us has a hand in it.

Can I get paid to take out my grass?

Yes, and winter is a smart time to plan it. Removing grass and planting water-wise plants is best done in the spring, so applying now puts you in line before the busy season.

Washington County

The Washington County Water Conservancy District’s Water Efficient Landscape Program pays $3 for each square foot of grass replaced with water-efficient landscaping. KUTV reported in June 2026 that the $3 rate covers the first 1,000 square feet, with $2 a square foot after that, so a typical 1,400-square-foot project would get about $3,800, and projects need to be finished before June 1, 2027 (KUTV). There’s also a $100-per-tree “Treebate” for approved trees planted in the converted area (FOX 13).

Two big rules: don’t start tearing out grass until your application is approved and the district has done a site visit, or you won’t qualify. And the program records a conservation easement on the converted area, so read the agreement before you sign. Questions go to the district at 435-673-3617.

Cedar City and Iron County

Cedar City and unincorporated Iron County are on the state’s list of communities eligible for Utah Water Savers landscape rebates. The statewide program lists lawn replacement at $1.50 to $3.00 per square foot depending on where you live, plus drip conversion, smart controller, and tree rebates (Slow the Flow). When Cedar City first became eligible in 2023, the local rate was $1.50 a square foot (Central Iron County Water Conservancy District). Confirm the current amount on the Slow the Flow site before you plan around it.

Does a water-wise yard help when it’s time to sell?

From what I see at showings, buyers here have gotten very used to desert landscaping. A tidy, low-water front yard reads as “low maintenance” and “lower bills,” and that’s exactly what a lot of our buyers, especially retirees and second-home owners, want. A patchy, brown lawn in a front yard does the opposite. If you ever do decide to sell, I wrote more about curb appeal in what to do before listing your home.

And if you’re in Washington County, keep an eye on HOA rules too. Some neighborhoods have their own landscape standards, so check before you pull up sod.

Frequently asked questions

When should I blow out my sprinklers in Cedar City? Before the first hard freeze, which on average is late September. If it isn’t done yet, get it done as soon as possible.

Do I need to winterize sprinklers in St. George? Many homeowners don’t fully blow out their lines, but you should still protect backflow valves, exposed pipes, and outdoor faucets. Short cold snaps happen most winters.

How many days a week should I water in St. George in November? Up to one day a week, per the Washington County Water Conservancy District, and most plants need no water at all in December and January.

How much does Washington County pay to remove grass? Up to $3 per square foot through the Water Efficient Landscape Program, plus $100 per approved tree. Apply and get approved before you remove anything.

Can Cedar City residents get a grass removal rebate? Yes. Cedar City is eligible for the statewide Utah Water Savers program. Check slowtheflow.org for the current rate.

Will removing my lawn hurt my home’s value? In my experience, a well-done water-wise yard is a plus with Southern Utah buyers. Every home is different, so call me if you want a second opinion before a big project.
